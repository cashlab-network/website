What changed between 422 and 423

Everything in 422's "what changed" list ran through this epoch — our own node as primary RPC, proven fallbacks on every critical source, work-based health checks, off-site alerting. Two new feeds (SGB/USD and USDX/USD) shipped mid-epoch and hit the accuracy band in essentially every round they were live for; epoch 424 is their first full epoch.

What happened

Our RPC layer had a single point of failure. A metered third-party endpoint we depended on was disabled mid-epoch over a billing overage — our oversight — causing 14 consecutive hours of zero submissions (Aug 9, 07:00–20:00 UTC). Outside that window our FTSO hit rate was ~89.8%; the outage alone dragged it under the gate. Separately, a health check that trusted "process running" over "work coming out" hid a dead Ethereum data source for roughly two days, which is what pushed FDC participation down to 35.6%.

What changed since — all live and tested

  • Our own Flare node is now the primary RPC for every component, with automatic failover through a monitored pool — a dead endpoint rotates out within ~6 minutes.
  • Every critical data source now has a proven fallback, including the XRP feed (89% of FDC rounds) — validated by live drill, not by assumption.
  • Health checks now require proven work (a real verified transaction, a climbing submission counter), not just a running process.
  • Independent off-site monitoring alerts us even if our servers die entirely.
  • Feed-accuracy improvements measured and merged (+1.44pp), with more in test.
