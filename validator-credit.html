Self-Repaying Validator Credit

Validators sell reward FLR to pay their bills — a sell flow working against the FIRE buyback. Flare has already shipped the primitives to replace that selling with credit. This is the design.

EVERY CLAIM VERIFIED AGAINST CHAIN AND SOURCE · 2026-08-26

01 · The problem

Operator costs are real, and selling rewards is the obvious way to pay them

Every validator has real costs — servers, bandwidth, time — and an operator without outside income has one obvious way to fund them: selling reward FLR on-market. FIP.16 — accepted by governance in April 2026 with 98.06% support — attacks sell pressure from the revenue side (FIRE buys back and burns; inflation drops 5%→3%). Operator selling works against it every epoch.

Telling operators "just don't sell" isn't a mechanism. Giving them something cheaper than selling is — and it can be assembled almost entirely from contracts already live on Flare mainnet.

02 · The insight

Flare has already shipped the pieces of a credit stack

A track record nobody can fake, published by the network itself. Built-in permission for a wallet to let a contract collect its rewards and pass them along. A price feed run by the validators themselves. Everything a lender needs — proof of income, a way to get repaid, and a fair price — already lives on-chain.

03 · The mechanism

Borrow stables against the stream. Rewards retire the loan. Nothing touches the order book.

Sizing — the dual cap

line = min( 70% × trailing stream × term , 50% × margin value )
In plain terms: borrow up to 70% of what your rewards are on track to earn during the loan — but never more than half the value of the collateral you put up. The first cushion covers bad weeks; the second covers price drops.

Underwriting — the pass ledger is a credit score

Flare publishes every validator's report card — passes, strikes, uptime, eligibility — every 3.5 days, where anyone can check it. Loan terms read straight from that report card:

The floating pass-rate

Interest reprices every epoch from the borrower's live pass count — hold 3/3 passes and pay ~6%; take a strike and drift toward 9% until the record heals. In short: the better you run your validator, the cheaper your money — recalculated every 3.5 days from public records. It also hands validators a direct cash incentive to maintain exactly the discipline the network already wants.

No liquidation price — a state trigger

Settlement-only seizure — the anti-predation rule

On default the vault takes exactly the balance, accrued interest, and a fixed default fee. Every remaining token returns to the borrower. Lenders are assured recovery up to margin, never enrichment — so no party ever profits from causing a borrower to fail. This forecloses the loan-to-own attack class by construction, and it is why the credit prices cheap: near-certain recovery prices like secured lending, not venture debt.

04 · Lender economics

A DCA machine that pays you to use it

The lender this is built for is someone who wants to build up a FLR position anyway. Put in stablecoins; receive the borrowers' FLR rewards in small batches every 3.5 days, at the oracle's fair price — building a position without moving the market or paying an exchange spread — plus interest on top. If a borrower defaults, the collateral pays you back, up to its value. The risk you keep is FLR's price while you accumulate: the same risk anyone buying FLR takes, with ~6–9% yearly interest added. Positions can be sold to another lender mid-term.

05 · Stated limitations

We'd rather you hear these from us

Six known limitations, and what cures each
  1. The income pledge isn't ironclad on normal wallets. Owners can always self-claim around the vault, so v1 is honestly a margin-backed loan whose normal path is self-repayment — the innovation is the UX and the underwriting data. Cured in v2 by claim-binding accounts.
  2. Everyone's rewards can change at once. A network-wide reward change shrinks every stream at once — FIP.16's accepted restructuring is reshaping reward economics now, and future governance can again. Mitigated by short terms (the book reprices each roll), reserves, and conservative haircuts.
  3. Rewards expire if nobody claims them (~90 days). The keeper must claim on schedule; redundant keepers required. Structurally solved by v2 enrollment.
  4. The market starts small. Flare's validator set alone is a niche book — the cross-chain roadmap is where the market becomes large.
  5. The insurance layer is new. Cover terms must be pre-agreed with Firelight; open design item, not a solved one.
  6. Lending has legal weight. The operator needs real counsel — and CashLab is explicitly not volunteering to operate or custody.

06 · Roadmap

Each phase names exactly what it builds on — and what it still needs

Why this serves Flare

  • Deletes operator sell pressure at the source — the complement to FIRE: revenue removes FLR; this keeps operator flow from ever reaching the market.
  • Pays validators to maintain pass discipline via the floating rate.
  • Creates organic premium demand for Firelight's cover market.
  • Gives FAssets and the FDC a concrete use case.
  • Retains small validators — the operators for whom opex-versus-selling is a monthly dilemma.
